A small box that listens

Talk instead
of typing.

Hold the button, say what you mean, let go. The words appear on whatever you're using — phone, laptop, anything with a screen. Nothing you say ever leaves the device.

Each mode shows what the real device produces from the same spoken sentence.

Why it exists

Speaking is faster than typing. Tidying up afterwards is not.

Ordinary dictation hands you a wall of words with every “um” and false start still in it, so you spend the time you saved cleaning it up. This cleans as it goes — and can go further, turning a rambling thought into something organised without dropping anything you said.

It also waits for you. Pause mid-sentence to think for as long as you like. It only stops listening when you let go.

Three things worth knowing

Nothing leaves the box

Your voice is never uploaded. Hearing you, writing it down, tidying it up — all of it happens on the device. It works with the internet unplugged.

It pretends to be a keyboard

Your phone or laptop just sees a wireless keyboard. No app, no account, no permissions. If a device accepts a keyboard, it accepts this.

It works anywhere you type

Not one blessed app. A text box, a spreadsheet cell, a chat window, a search bar — wherever the cursor is, that's where the words go.

Using it

The whole thing is one button.

HOLD

Press and keep holding. A light comes on to show it's listening.

SPEAK

Say the thing. Pause, backtrack, think — it keeps listening.

RELEASE

Let go. A moment later the words appear where your cursor is.

You can also just talk to it. Say “organised mode” to change how it writes, or “press enter” to press Enter. It knows the difference between an instruction and a sentence that merely sounds like one.

Being honest

What it isn't.

  • It isn't instant. A few seconds pass between letting go and the words landing. It's doing real work on a very small computer.
  • It isn't an assistant. It doesn't answer questions or do things for you. It writes down what you said.
  • It isn't perfect at names. Unusual words get misheard, like any dictation. You can teach it the ones you use often.
  • It talks to one device at a time. Moving from laptop to phone means disconnecting one and connecting the other.
