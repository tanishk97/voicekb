A small box that listens

Talk instead of typing.

Hold the button, say what you mean, let go. The words appear wherever your cursor is — an email, a message, a document, a search box. Nothing you say ever leaves the device.

Why it exists

Speaking is faster than typing. Reading back what you said is not.

Dictation usually gives you a wall of words with every “um” and false start still in it, so you spend the time you saved cleaning it up. This tidies as it goes — and it can go further, turning a rambling thought into something organised, without dropping anything you said.

It also waits for you. Pause mid-sentence to think for as long as you like; it only stops listening when you let go of the button.

Three things worth knowing

Nothing leaves the box

Your voice is never uploaded. Everything — hearing you, writing it down, tidying it up — happens on the device itself. It works with the internet unplugged.

It pretends to be a keyboard

Your phone or laptop sees an ordinary wireless keyboard. No app to install, no account, no permissions. If a device accepts a keyboard, it accepts this.

It works anywhere you type

Not one blessed app. Whatever is in front of you — a text box, a spreadsheet cell, a chat window, a search bar — is where the words go.

Using it

The whole interaction is one button.

HOLD

Press and keep holding. A light comes on to show it's listening.

SPEAK

Say the thing. Pause, backtrack, think — it keeps listening.

RELEASE

Let go. A few seconds later the words appear where your cursor is.

You can also just talk to it. Saying “organised mode” switches how it writes; “press enter” presses Enter. It knows the difference between an instruction and a sentence that happens to sound like one.

What it is made of

A Raspberry Pi, a microphone, and a button.

No subscription, no cloud service, nothing to sign up for. It's a small computer that costs about as much as a nice dinner, running software that is free and open, doing one job well.

If you want to build one, the full instructions — parts, wiring, setup — are in the repository.

Being honest

What it isn't.

  • It isn't instant. Expect a few seconds between letting go and the words appearing. It's doing the work on a very small computer.
  • It isn't a smart assistant. It doesn't answer questions or do things for you. It writes down what you said.
  • It isn't perfect at names. Unusual words and made-up product names get misheard, like any dictation. You can teach it the ones you use often.
  • It talks to one device at a time. Switching from your laptop to your phone means disconnecting one and connecting the other.
